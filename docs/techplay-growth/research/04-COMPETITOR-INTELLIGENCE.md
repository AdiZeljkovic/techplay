# 04 — Competitor Intelligence

Status: Phase 1 research draft — 27 Sep 2026
Scope: 21 named gaming and tech publications, plus about 40 databases, trackers, tools, aggregators and new independent outlets. The focus is acquisition: how each one gets and keeps an audience, not only what it publishes.
Structure: an overall summary and acquisition matrix first, then three parts. Part A (IGN, GameSpot, PC Gamer, GamesRadar+, Polygon, Eurogamer, Rock Paper Shotgun) was written for this file from pages fetched on 27 Sep 2026. Part B (Kotaku, Game Rant, TheGamer, Dexerto, DualShockers, VG247, Push Square, Nintendo Life, Pure Xbox) and Part C (GamingBolt, Insider Gaming, Wccftech, TechRadar, Tom's Hardware, and the databases and trackers) are the research agents' write-ups. Machine-readable matrix: `competitor-matrix.csv`.

No traffic figure in this file is measured by TechPlay. Where a number appears it is the company's own claim, a public-company filing, or a counter shown on the competitor's page, and it is labelled that way.

## Executive summary

1. **FACT — Gaming media is contracting and consolidating.** IGN cut staff in September 2026 (Aftermath, 14 Sep 2026). GameSpot cut staff in July 2026 (Aftermath, 9 Jul 2026). Eurogamer cut editorial staff in February 2026 (VGC). Vox sold Polygon to Valnet in May 2025 (Game Developer). Future plc, which owns PC Gamer, GamesRadar+, TechRadar and Tom's Hardware, reported profit before tax down 67% in the half to 31 Mar 2026, with Google Search and Discover traffic down about 20% and 60% of revenue on Google-dependent brands (PPC Land, 14 May 2026).
2. **FACT — The large publishers are all building logged-in products.** IGN sells IGN Plus ($4.99 a month or $29.99 a year) and runs free Rewards, Daily Games, Playlist lists and interactive maps. Future runs a free "Future+" membership and badges ("Earn badges by reading articles, leaving comments, and taking quizzes" on PC Gamer). Eurogamer and Rock Paper Shotgun sell ad-free supporter tiers ($2.99 a month at Eurogamer). Valnet sells Premium at $5.99 a month. Hookshot sells Supporter at $3.99 a month.
3. **FACT — Everyone is building a game database, and none imports your library.** Valnet bought OpenCritic and launched the Game Rant database. Dexerto has a database and 80+ wikis. Hookshot runs per-platform game pages with Have / Want / Maybe / Nope collection states. HowLongToBeat, now an IGN brand, imports Steam only. TechPlay already imports Steam, PlayStation, Xbox, GOG and Epic for free (Part C §2).
4. **FACT — Guide volume at Valnet is industrial.** Game Rant listed 73 guides and 7 news articles for Silent Hill: Townfall three days after launch (Part B). A two-author newsroom cannot and should not compete on that axis.
5. **FACT — Community rituals beat ratings.** Push Square and Pure Xbox had 0–1 user ratings for Control Resonant three days after launch, while one Nintendo Life poll drew 9,331 votes (Part B). GamesRadar+ runs a monthly RPG Club and a clip contest ("GamesRadar+ Replay", two $250/£250 Amazon vouchers a month).
6. **FACT — The new wave is small, owned and reader-funded.** Aftermath, Second Wind, Rogue, Respec, Game File, Post Games and a bought-back Giant Bomb all launched or re-launched since late 2023 (Part C).
7. **OBSERVATION — No browser-push code was found on 15 publisher homepages checked (Parts A and B, GameSpot excluded), and few put Discord on the homepage.** Insider Gaming and Eurogamer are the exceptions for Discord links; RPS lists Discord in its nav.
8. **HYPOTHESIS — TechPlay's defensible position is the combination nobody has:** a cross-platform library that fills itself, a game database it owns rather than rents, a Discord bot wired to the site, and a small independent newsroom that can say plainly who owns it. Every competitor has one or two of these, not all four.

## Part A — IGN, GameSpot, PC Gamer, GamesRadar+, Polygon, Eurogamer, Rock Paper Shotgun

Written from pages fetched on 27 Sep 2026: homepages, a news article, a guide, a review, game or hub pages, newsletter and membership pages, YouTube channel pages, and ownership and layoff reports. GameSpot's own pages returned a Cloudflare challenge; its section relies on its video RSS, the Wayback Machine and third-party reports.

### A.1 IGN (Ziff Davis)

- **FACT — Logged-in product ladder.** IGN Plus costs $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year for "No ads, free games, and discounts". IGN Rewards offers "free to claim offers, discounts and giveaways with an IGN account", for example 10% off Humble Choice.
- **FACT — Tools and utilities.** Interactive maps for many games; IGN Daily Games ("Play today's daily game and challenge your knowledge"); IGN Playlist, a public list feature with community lists such as "Tony's Tiers"; community-editable wikis ("Contribute yourself as well!").
- **FACT — Game pages.** Each game has a hub titled "[Game] – Release Date, Trailers, News, Reviews, Guides, Gameplay and more", and an "Upcoming Games" release list.
- **FACT — Google prompts.** Articles carry an "Add IGN on Google" link, which is Google's preferred-sources feature.
- **FACT — Comments are heavy on big stories.** IGN's GTA 6 price article showed 1,248 comments.
- **FACT — Ownership and cuts.** Ziff Davis owns IGN, Eurogamer, VG247, HowLongToBeat and Maxroll (Part C and Wikipedia). IGN laid off staff in September 2026 (Aftermath) after an earlier round in August 2025 (Game Developer).
- **What works (OBSERVATION):** a free account that returns something (rewards, lists, maps) plus a paid ad-free tier.
- **Learn:** logged-in utilities that make sense for one game (maps, checklists), and the "Add us on Google" prompt.
- **Do not copy:** the scale of wikis and video. TechPlay cannot staff them.
- **Vulnerability:** repeated layoffs during the busiest season; guides spread over hundreds of games.

### A.2 GameSpot (Fandom)

- **FACT — Fetch blocked.** GameSpot's pages returned a Cloudflare challenge to the research fetcher. Its video RSS feed was readable.
- **FACT — Cuts.** Aftermath reported "Layoffs Hit GameSpot Again" on 9 Jul 2026. Fandom itself went through another round of layoffs (TheWrap).
- **FACT — Headline format this week** (from its RSS): data and explainer framing, for example "GTA 6 $400 Collector's Edition Comes With Everything Except The Game", "Meta's $1,300 VR Glasses Could Be The Most Expensive Way To Play Xbox Games".
- **FACT — Evergreen lists rank.** "Best Open-World Games To Explore In 2026" and "The Best Games Of 2026 (So Far)" appeared on Bing page one (03).
- **UNKNOWN:** account proposition, newsletter cadence, YouTube size (the extracted figure was inconsistent and is not reported).
- **Learn:** dated "best of the year so far" lists as evergreen anchors.

### A.3 PC Gamer (Future plc)

- **FACT — Hyperfeed.** An "experimental new homepage layout with fresh features, raw thoughts from our editors, and interactive polls" that invites feedback ("27 Comments").
- **FACT — Badges.** "Earn badges by reading articles, leaving comments, and taking quizzes" for signed-in members (Future+).
- **FACT — Living pages.** "Epic Games Store current free games" is "an up-to-date list of what's currently being given away, and the entire history". "Best graphics cards in 2026" is a first-person tested buying guide.
- **FACT — Newsletter and edition switcher** (US, Canada, UK, Australia).
- **Learn:** polls on the homepage; living list pages that are updated rather than re-published; first-person "I tested this" voice in buying guides.
- **Do not copy:** hardware buying guides before TechPlay tests hardware.

### A.4 GamesRadar+ (Future plc)

- **FACT — Community formats.** "GamesRadar+ RPG Club (September 2026): The Blood of Dawnwalker" with discussion in the comments. "GamesRadar+ Replay" lets readers submit clips, with two $250/£250 Amazon vouchers each month.
- **FACT — Awards.** Runs the Golden Joystick Awards; voting was open on 27 Sep 2026.
- **FACT — GTA 6 franchise page.** "Countdown to GTA 6 with GamesRadar+ … join GamesRadar+ weekly as we explore GTA 6", with a newsletter sign-up gate.
- **FACT — Evergreen lists.** "Upcoming Switch 2 games for 2026 and beyond"; "25 Best open world games to play in 2026" ranked first on Bing for its query (03).
- **FACT — Review policy page** ("GamesRadar Review Scores") and a weekly newsletter ("Weekly digests, tales from the communities you love").
- **Learn:** a monthly game club is cheap and TechPlay has a forum and Discord to host it; a clip contest is a UGC loop that also produces social content.
- **Do not copy:** the free-membership page that points to a football brand's community (the membership page fetched was FourFourTwo's, which shows Future+ is shared across brands).

### A.5 Polygon (Valnet since May 2025)

- **FACT — Ownership.** Vox sold Polygon to Valnet (Game Developer, 2 May 2025).
- **FACT — Guide hubs.** "Subnautica 2 guides and walkthroughs" collects every guide for a game on one page. The nav leads with Zelda, GTA 6, Guides and Pokémon.
- **FACT — Follow and threads.** Pages show "Follow / Followed" and a comment "Thread" count, and ask readers to sign in to comment.
- **FACT — Prediction content.** "Game Awards 2026: All Game of the Year Front-Runners and Predictions", 48 follows at fetch time.
- **Learn:** per-game guide hubs; awards-prediction posts two months before The Game Awards.
- **Do not copy:** the post-sale drift into TV and anime verticals.

### A.6 Eurogamer (Ziff Davis)

- **FACT — Supporter tier.** "Ad-free browsing experience, supporter-only articles and videos … only £2.99/$2.99 a month", or $29.99 a year, with a pink "supporter" badge in comments and game-key giveaways for yearly subscribers.
- **FACT — Commerce.** A Eurogamer Store selling "official digital codes", a deals section, and "Upcoming Video Game Releases" guides.
- **FACT — Social footprint in footer:** Bluesky, Discord, Facebook, TikTok, X and YouTube; regional editions in Spanish, Polish and Portuguese.
- **FACT — Cuts.** "IGN-owned Eurogamer cuts editorial staff" (VGC, 26 Feb 2026).
- **Learn:** the supporter badge in comments is recognition that costs nothing; key giveaways as a subscriber perk.
- **Do not copy:** selling codes before an audience exists.

### A.7 Rock Paper Shotgun (Ziff Davis)

- **FACT — Supporter tier.** "Ad-free browsing, our monthly letter from the editor, and discounts on RPS merch", with a supporter archive.
- **FACT — Nav includes Discord, Deals, Podcasts, RSS, "Free games" and "Bestest Bests".** "The best PC games to play right now" shows "Updated on June 19, 2026" and 39 comments.
- **FACT — Weekly ritual.** "The Sunday Papers" link round-up appears on the homepage.
- **Learn:** a named weekly ritual and a visible "Updated on" date on evergreen lists.

### A.8 Synthesis for Part A

- **OBSERVATION — The big publishers now compete on logged-in features** (IGN Plus and Rewards, Future+ badges, supporter tiers). They agree that a reader who signs in is worth more; Future's own figure is 4× longer sessions for known users (Part C §1.4).
- **OBSERVATION — Their community is comments plus one or two rituals** (RPG Club, Sunday Papers, clip contest), not Discord-first.
- **OBSERVATION — They are losing staff while trying to hold search share,** which leaves slow-moving evergreen pages (series order, where-to-play, PC fixes) less defended than before (03, 09).

### B.Executive summary

1. FACT: The nine sites fall into four ownership blocs — Valnet (Game Rant, TheGamer, DualShockers; Montreal; also OpenCritic, Polygon, Fextralife, Hardcore Gamer), Hookshot Media (Push Square, Nintendo Life, Pure Xbox; UK, founder-majority, Ziff Davis minority stake, IGN as ad-sales partner), Keleops (Kotaku, Swiss/European, since 2 Jul 2025), IGN Entertainment/Ziff Davis (VG247 via Gamer Network Ltd), plus independent Dexerto Ltd (London, privately held).
2. FACT: VG247 is no longer a news site in practice. Its news archive's newest item is dated 2 Jun 2026; its RSS feed (100 items) spans 25 Nov 2025 – 2 Jun 2026 (ESTIMATE 0.5 items/day); only the guides archive is alive (newest 27 Sep 2026) and it consists of Roblox/Monopoly Go/gacha "codes" pages. VGC reported (26 Feb 2026) the editorial team was moved to Eurogamer in summer 2025 and the brand now runs as "a small guides operation run by a pair of full-time writers". The brand is a cautionary tale, not a competitor.
3. FACT: Valnet's guide volume is real and extreme: Game Rant's database entry for Silent Hill: Townfall (released 24 Sep 2026) listed **73 guides and 7 news articles three days after launch**; Control Resonant (same release day) had 48 Game Rant guides and 15+ TheGamer guides. Game Rant's guides feed on 27 Sep was 16/20 items about The Witcher 3, pre-loading for the 29 Sep Remastered release. This is done by a paid guides team on quotas; in May 2026 Valnet moved TheGamer contributors to "Pay Per Session" ($3/$5/$8 per 1,000 sessions in a 15-day window, no minimum), after October 2025 layoffs. Do not copy the volume; copy the *structure* (one hub per game, guides linked from it, pre-loaded before release) for one or two games at a time.
4. FACT: Hookshot's model is the closest to TechPlay's: a per-platform game database (release dates by region, publisher, players, genre, accessibility filters, subscription filters), a 10-point critic score next to a 10-point user rating, per-user collection states **Have / Want / Maybe / Nope!**, tabs Overview / Reviews / Screenshots / Guides / News / Where To Buy, native comments, weekly community rituals ("What Are You Playing This Weekend?", "Box Art Brawl"), polls, forums, and a $3.99/mo–$39.99/yr ad-free Supporter tier with an explicit "no paywall" pledge.
5. OBSERVATION: Even at Hookshot's scale (Nintendo Life: 852k YouTube subscribers, 264k Instagram; Push Square: 172k YouTube, 189.2k Facebook) per-game user ratings are thin — Control Resonant had **1** user rating on Push Square and **0** on Pure Xbox three days after launch; a 1988 Castlevania entry had 28. Where their community actually shows up is comments (115–210 on big reviews/lists/news) and polls (5,106 and 9,331 votes on one Nintendo Direct round-up). Lesson for TechPlay: do not expect ratings to fill a 333k-game database; design for polls, lists and collections first.
6. ESTIMATE (RSS, 27 Sep 2026, Sunday): publishing cadence — Kotaku ~6.8 items/day; Dexerto ~17/day; Push Square ~18/day (weekday sample); Nintendo Life ~10/day; Pure Xbox ~8/day; TheGamer 10 items in 7.7 hours; Game Rant 10 items in 3.0 hours; DualShockers 10 items in 5.3 hours (Valnet feeds only return 10 items, so per-day figures for them are extrapolations, likely 30–80/day on a Sunday).
7. FACT: Every site except Dexerto and VG247 sells or offers a membership: Valnet "Premium" $5.99/mo or $49.99/yr (ad-free + "Member exclusive" stories — a partial paywall); Hookshot "Supporter" $3.99/mo or $39.99/yr across all five Hookshot sites, ad-free only, "We have no plans pay-wall our content". Kotaku has none observed; it has Amazon-led "Deals" (about 70% general tech) and a Keleops software "Downloads" hub bolted onto the nav.
8. OBSERVATION: Nobody in this group was seen running browser push notifications (no OneSignal/PushEngage/webpushr/Firebase messaging signatures in any of the nine homepages). Community is native comments (Hookshot, Kotaku on WordPress, Valnet "Threads" with a legacy Disqus loader) plus forums (Hookshot) — no Discord invite or subreddit link was present on any of the nine homepages.
9. RECOMMENDATION (headline): TechPlay should position against Hookshot's biggest structural gap — Hookshot is siloed per platform (three sites, three logins, plus a nascent Steam Hype), while TechPlay's one cross-platform library already exists. Borrow Hookshot's per-game page grammar and community rituals, Valnet's per-game hub-and-guides structure at 1/50th the volume, Game Rant's "Add us as a preferred source on Google" prompt, and Dexerto's stated AI policy as a trust asset; avoid Valnet's labour model, Kotaku's off-topic deals, Dexerto's viral framing, and VG247's codes farm.

- DIFFERENTIATION: TechPlay can attach every news item to a game page and a library state (Have/Want) — Kotaku cannot.

### Economics behind it (FACT from Aftermath 25 May 2026, Kotaku 23 May 2026, Wikipedia)
- Pay Per Session at TheGamer: $3 (editors) / $5 (edited articles) / $8 (self-published) per 1,000 sessions in the first 15 days; no minimum; Valnet's pitch "earn up to $2,500 per article based on traffic performance"; workers: "heartbreaking", "soft-layoffs", "open revolt". October 2025 layoffs "decimated" TheGamer's editorial team.
- TheWrap (Mar 2025) allegations: Collider freelance rates $250 → $40 per article after acquisition; review rates $150 → $30 across Valnet sites; a 400-name blacklist (allegations; Valnet's response not fetched — UNVERIFIED).
- Valnet's April 2026 vendor figures: Game Rant 28M visits, TheGamer 14M, DualShockers 2M, Fextralife 28M visits/84M pageviews, OpenCritic 1M; "750 million unique readers in the past year" across the gaming portfolio. Kotaku's framing of the same period: written-word sites "hemorrhaging hits" from Google changes and AI search.

### Why it is dangerous for a small site (RECOMMENDATION with reasoning)
1. It only pays at scale. At $5 per 1,000 sessions a guide needs ~20,000 sessions to earn a $100 freelance fee; TechPlay measured 1–2 search clicks/day across the whole site since the 17 Aug 2026 Cloudflare incident. The unit economics are not a rounding error apart, they are a different business.
2. It is the segment most exposed to AI Overviews — "how long to beat", "all endings", "how to romance Yennefer" are precisely the answers a search engine now gives inline. Kotaku's quote and VG247's collapse into codes pages are the visible symptoms.
3. The reputational cost is now attached to the *format*: readers and journalists associate 73-guides-in-three-days with content-mill labour (TheWrap, Aftermath, Kotaku coverage). A small site that mimics the shape inherits the suspicion without the traffic.
4. It requires a database to be worth anything — the guides are valuable to Valnet because they populate GRDB and lists (DualShockers' 10-entry list = 10 database boxes = 10 store-link chances). Volume without the hub is just a blog.

### What TechPlay can take from it (RECOMMENDATION, concrete)
- The **hub-first** shape: TechPlay's `/games/[slug]` should list its own guides/news/reviews for that game (Hookshot and GRDB both do this). Check whether `Article` ↔ `Game` linkage exists in the backend (`game_id` or polymorphic relation on articles) — UNKNOWN from this research; if absent it is the single highest-leverage schema change for the content side.
- **Bounded launch clusters**: for at most one or two games per month that TechPlay's writers actually play, publish 5–8 guides in launch week using TheGamer's skeleton (TOC, question H2s, quick answer, sibling links, updated date). Push Square's 12-page Wolverine day shows a small team can do it for one title.
- **Pre-load predictable spikes** with content TechPlay already has: GTA 6 hub (121 vehicles / 36 weapons / 12 characters / 1,058 locations) is exactly a pre-loaded guide cluster; keep its pages dated and "Updated" as facts confirm.
- **Preferred Source prompt**: Valnet sites ask readers to "Add Us" as a preferred source on Google — free, and it directly affects Top Stories exposure. Verify Google's current eligibility rules by search before implementing (gap).

## Where competitors are vulnerable

1. **Staff cuts during peak season.** IGN (Sep 2026), GameSpot (Jul 2026), Eurogamer (Feb 2026), TheGamer (Oct 2025), Fandom. Slow-to-update evergreen pages are the first casualty. OBSERVATION: page one for PC-fix queries already has no major outlet (09).
2. **No cross-platform library.** Hookshot is siloed by platform; GRDB, Backloggd and Stash are manual; HowLongToBeat imports Steam only; paid trackers charge for PSN and Xbox import (Part C §2.8).
3. **Rented data.** Backloggd, Gamery and Playnite all run on IGDB. TechPlay owns its rows and can correct and enrich them (Part C §2.4).
4. **Search dependence with no owned channel.** GamingBolt has no email, account, comments or Discord. Future says 60% of revenue sits on Google-dependent brands.
5. **Trust erosion.** Valnet's pay-per-session contracts, VG247's codes farm, AI controversies. A small outlet that publishes who owns it and how it uses AI stands out (Part B §12).

## Where TechPlay could differentiate (RECOMMENDATION)

1. **"Your library, filled for you, across every platform, free."** No competitor in the sample offers this without payment (Part C executive summary, point 3).
2. **Per-game pages that answer the player's question with the player's own data:** "you own this on Steam", "your friends played it", "it's on your Game Pass", "it's 40% off".
3. **Polls, clubs and lists over ratings**, borrowing Hookshot's and GamesRadar+'s rituals (Part B §12; A.4).
4. **An ownership, funding and AI-use page.** Costs nothing; Hookshot and Dexerto show the format.
5. **A Discord bot tied to site accounts.** SteamDB and Insider Gaming run Discords; none of the publishers links site XP, library and Discord the way Professor Buffy already does.

## What TechPlay should not copy

- Valnet's guide volume and pay-per-session labour model.
- Codes pages, daily puzzle answers, and general-tech deals.
- Paywalls before an audience exists (Tom's Hardware Premium, MobyGames Pro).
- Platform sub-sites.
- Leak-driven news without a source network.
- Browser push as a differentiator: no push-vendor code was found on the 15 publisher homepages checked (Part B §12; Part A checked separately, GameSpot blocked). It may still be useful for release reminders, which is a product question (12).
